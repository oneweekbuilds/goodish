About

Who builds this, and why.

AlgorithmLens is built by one person, Justin Winuk, in Somerville, Massachusetts. It started from a plain question: what was actually in my feed this week? A feed keeps no record of itself, so the only way to answer was to make one.

The app is a counting instrument. It reads a short recording of a feed you already scroll and reports what appeared: which posts carried a printed ad label, which accounts came around again, what the platform marked as suggested. It stops where the screen stops. The method is published, the limits sit beside every number, and the example data on this site is fictional and says so.

What it will never do

Claim to know why a platform showed you a post.
Score you, streak you, or grade your habits.
Store the text of posts, or sell anything about you.
Run ads on the platforms it measures.
Present a guess as an observation.

The loop

Record a few minutes. Review what appeared. Record again another week, and the two scans sit side by side. Nothing in the loop looks at the platform's model, because nothing can.

Nothing here reads the platform's model. The loop only ever sees the screen.
