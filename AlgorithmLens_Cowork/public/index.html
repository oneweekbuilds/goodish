04The limits

What a scan can read. What it cannot.

A recording holds what was on the screen. The report stops where the screen stops.

Can read

  • an ad label on a post
  • an account name
  • a post's position in the feed
  • the date recorded
  • how many posts appeared
  • an account that returns
  • a suggested label
  • a following label
  • a headline on screen

Cannot read

  • why a post appeared
  • your beliefs
  • your mood
  • the platform's model
  • what you scrolled past
  • which tap led where
  • the whole feed
  • the next feed
  • who else saw it

We show what appeared in a sample and keep observed labels apart from inferences. Political content describes a post, not your beliefs. Tone describes a reading of the content, not your mood. A suggested post does not tell us which action led to the recommendation.

The examples on this page are fictional: not a live scan, and not a demonstration of a running classifier. The one real scan is marked as such. This page does not record your screen or analyze your feed.

09Questions

Fair questions, short answers.

Each answer comes from the privacy policy, the Terms or the methodology, and says which.

Does it see my messages and notifications?

It can, if they are on your screen while you record. A scan is a recording of the screen, so a notification or a message that appears during it is captured along with the feed. Those frames are sent to Google's Gemini API to be read by software, like any others, then deleted from your phone, and no person reviews them. Close sensitive apps before you start a scan.

From the privacy policy, section 1.2.

Who receives the recording?

Google, and no one else. When you approve the analysis, the frames (compressed images of your screen and the text recognized on them) go from your phone to Google's Gemini API to be read. They are then deleted from your phone, and AlgorithmLens keeps no copy of them: what is saved to your account is the results as text, not images. If you separately join the optional research study, the text read from each post, never an image, is also sent to a second service, TypeSafe AI.

From the privacy policy, section 3, and the methodology, where the data goes.

Is it really free?

Yes. The Terms say AlgorithmLens is currently free to use, with no subscriptions, no paid tiers and no in-app purchases, and that it does not process payments. If paid features are ever introduced, the Terms will be updated with the pricing before any charge applies. The privacy policy says your data is not sold and is not used for advertising.

From the Terms, section 7, and the privacy policy, section 10.

Why TestFlight?

The app is still in public beta, and the beta is distributed through Apple's TestFlight. It runs on iPhone, iOS 15.1 and later. If you would rather wait for the App Store, the launch list below sends one email when the app is there.

From the Terms, section 1, and the methodology.

Is there an Android version?

No. AlgorithmLens is iPhone only for now. To hear if that changes, tick "Tell me about Android" on the launch list form below.

From the methodology, which describes an iPhone app in a TestFlight beta.
