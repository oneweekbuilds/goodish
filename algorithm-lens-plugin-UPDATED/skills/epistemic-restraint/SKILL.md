# Epistemic Restraint Standards for AlgorithmLens

AlgorithmLens documents what appeared in a social media feed. It never claims to explain why content appeared. All user-facing language must be descriptive, not accusatory or speculative.

## Core Principle

Describe observable composition. Never infer algorithmic intent.

## Banned Words and Phrases

Never use any of the following in user-facing text:

- "manipulate" / "manipulation"
- "trick" / "tricked"
- "brainwash" / "brainwashing"
- "exploit" / "exploitation"
- "propaganda"
- "pushed on you"
- "forced on you"
- "targeting you"
- "designed to make you"
- "the algorithm wants"
- "the algorithm is trying to"
- "secretly" / "hidden agenda"

## Acceptable vs. Unacceptable Framing

**Acceptable — describes what appeared:**
- "Your feed contained 34% suggested content"
- "Political content appeared in 12% of posts"
- "The top 3 sources accounted for 45% of your feed"
- "Sponsored content made up 8% of posts in this snapshot"
- "62% of your feed was content you didn't choose to follow"

**Unacceptable — implies intent or makes accusations:**
- "The algorithm is pushing political content on you"
- "You're being manipulated by suggested posts"
- "The platform is flooding your feed with ads"
- "Your feed is designed to keep you angry"
- "They're hiding content from accounts you follow"

## Tooltip and Explanatory Text Guidelines

- Clarify methodology: explain how categories are determined
- Acknowledge limits: note that categorization is approximate and based on observable signals
- Invite reflection: frame insights as information for the user's own interpretation
- Never tell users how to feel about their data

## Tone Requirements

- Calm, measured, informational
- Invite reflection, not outrage
- Present data without moral judgment
- Treat the user as intelligent and capable of drawing their own conclusions
